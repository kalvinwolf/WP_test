# Revolut Ultra (Spain) — Is It Worth It?

**Analysis date:** August 2026
**Profile assumed:** Spain resident, €1,500–2,500/month card spend, wants travel + medical cover, likes lounges/subs/cashback

---

## 1. First, an important correction

Revolut **does not sell private health insurance in Spain**. There is no Revolut equivalent of
Sanitas / Adeslas / DKV domestic cover.

What Revolut actually includes is **travel medical assistance** — emergency treatment while you are
abroad, outside your country of residence. It will not pay for a GP visit in Madrid, a specialist
consultation, or planned treatment in Spain.

If domestic private health cover is part of what you were buying, Ultra does not deliver it, and you
would still need a separate policy (roughly €50–90/month depending on age and insurer).

## 2. The plan lineup (Spain)

| Plan | Price | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | €0 | Baseline |
| Plus | €3.99/mo | Minor perks |
| Premium | €9.99/mo (~€120/yr) | **First tier with real travel insurance** |
| Metal | ~€13.99–15.99/mo, or **€155/yr** annually | Insurance + cashback + discounted lounges |
| Ultra | **€55/mo, or €540/yr paid annually** | Top tier |

Notes:
- Ultra was recently **raised from €45 to €55/month**, but the **annual price stayed at €540**. Paying
  annually is effectively €45/month and saves €120/yr versus monthly (€660). If you buy Ultra, buy it annually.
- Metal pricing appears inconsistent across sources (€13.99 vs €15.99) — confirm in-app.

## 3. Cashback — the weakest part of the case

The rates that matter:

- **0.1% within Europe**
- **1% outside Europe**
- Capped at **€50/month** on Ultra
- Does **not** apply to ATM withdrawals, transfers, or top-ups

Applied to your €1,500–2,500/month:

| Spend pattern | Monthly cashback | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| €1,500/mo, all in Europe | €1.50 | **€18** |
| €2,500/mo, all in Europe | €2.50 | **€30** |
| €2,000/mo, 75% Europe / 25% abroad | €6.50 | **€78** |
| €2,500/mo, 50% Europe / 50% abroad | €13.75 | **€165** |

Living in Spain, you are realistically in the **€20–80/year** band.

**Two conclusions:**
1. Cashback covers between **4% and 15%** of Ultra's €540 cost. Treat it as rounding, not a reason.
2. **Metal earns the same 0.1% / 1% rates.** So cashback gives Ultra *zero* advantage over a plan
   that costs a third as much. It should carry no weight in the Ultra-vs-Metal decision.

## 4. Insurance — what each tier actually gives you

| Feature | Premium (€9.99) | Metal (€155/yr) | Ultra (€540/yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency medical abroad | up to €10M | up to €10M | up to €10M |
| Emergency dental | €300 | €300 | €300 |
| Trip cancellation | up to €5,000/person | up to €5,000/person | "Cancel for any reason" |
| Lost/damaged baggage | ~€1,000 | ~€1,000 | ~€1,000 |
| Flight delay trigger | — | 8 hours | 4 hours |
| **Max trip length** | **30 days** | **30 days** | **90 days** |
| Who's covered | You + children | You + children + spouse/partner | You + children + spouse/partner |
| Winter sports | No | No | Yes |
| Car hire excess | No | No | Yes (~£2,000) |

**This is the crux of the whole decision.** The headline medical cover — the €10M emergency
treatment number that makes Ultra sound impressive — is **identical on Premium at €9.99/month**.

Ultra's genuine insurance upgrades are narrow:
- Trips longer than 30 days
- Cancel-for-any-reason (a real upgrade, but a niche one)
- 4h vs 8h delay trigger
- Skiing and rental-car excess

If none of those describe your travel, **you are paying €420/year extra for insurance you already had.**

## 5. Versus standalone travel insurance

A proper Spanish annual multi-trip policy:

| Option | Cost/yr | Medical limit | Max trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| IATI Anual Multiviaje | from **€209.99** | €300,000 worldwide | 90 days |
| Market range (IATI, Chapka, Intermundial, Mondo) | €150–400 | varies | 60–90 days |
| Revolut Premium | €120 | €10M | 30 days |
| Revolut Metal | €155 (annual billing) | €10M | 30 days |
| Revolut Ultra | €540 | €10M | 90 days |

The decisive comparison:

> **Metal (€155 annual) + a full IATI annual policy (€210) = ~€365/year.**
> That is **€175 cheaper than Ultra**, and gives you *better* insurance — a real standalone policy
> with a dedicated claims process, 90-day trips, and no dependence on card-linked activation rules.

A card-embedded policy is convenient but weaker in practice: geolocation/activation quirks,
pre-existing condition exclusions, and claims handled through an app chat rather than an insurer's
claims desk. If insurance is genuinely the reason you are considering Ultra, a standalone policy is
the better product *and* the cheaper one.

## 6. The non-insurance perks (other than lounges — see §7)

This is where Ultra's remaining case lives.

| Perk | Ultra's claim | Realistic value to you |
|---|---|---|
| Unlimited lounge access (1,400+) | Priority Pass Prestige = ~€430/yr | **Real, if you fly often** |
| Monthly eSIM data | ~€10–15/mo | Real if you travel outside EU roaming |
| Financial Times Premium | ~€39/mo claimed | Only if you'd actually subscribe |
| WeWork (3 days/mo) | ~€120/mo claimed | Only if you'd actually use it |
| NordVPN Complete | ~€15/mo claimed | ~€100/yr real market price |
| ClassPass (20 credits/mo) | ~€40/mo | Only if you'd actually use it |
| Tinder Gold, Uber One, Wolt | varies | Situational |

Revolut markets this as "€5,000+/year of value." **Ignore that number.** It is built from full list
prices of subscriptions almost nobody buys at list. The only honest way to value a bundled
subscription is: *what would I have paid for this anyway?* For most people that is one or two items,
not seven — and availability varies by country, so confirm which are live in Spain.

## 7. The Metal lounge discount — and why it hurts the Ultra case

Premium and Metal do not get free lounge access, but they do get a **"Revolut-funded discounted
rate"** on passes. Revolut does not publish the exact figure — it is shown in-app and varies by
lounge — but the reference points are:

- **LoungeKey standard rate: £22 (~€25)** — the benchmark Revolut discounts from
- **Walk-up rates: €30–50** at most lounges
- Third-party reports: ~$30–35/pass, described as 30–50% cheaper than walk-up

**Best estimate: 30–50% off, landing around €10–20 per visit.**

Terms to note: passes are **per person, per visit** (a travelling partner needs a second pass), valid
12 months from purchase, and require ID plus a same-day boarding pass. The discount is identical on
Premium and Metal — it is not a Metal-specific perk. Standard and Plus pay full price.

**This materially weakens the argument for Ultra.** If a Metal pass costs €10–20 rather than €30,
Ultra's unlimited access saves *less* per visit, so you need *more* flights to break even.

## 8. Break-even (revised)

The correct comparison is **Ultra vs Metal**, not Ultra vs nothing — because Metal already gives you
the medical cover, the cancellation cover, and the identical cashback.

```
Ultra (annual)   €540/yr
Metal (annual)   €155/yr
─────────────────────────
Delta to justify €385/yr
```

Lounge visits required to justify Ultra on lounges alone:

| If a Metal pass costs | Visits/yr needed |
|---|---|
| €10 | **~38** |
| €15 | **~26** |
| €20 | **~19** |
| €25 (best case) | **~15** |

Even on the most generous assumption you need **~15 visits/year** — 7–8 return trips using a lounge
at both ends. At a realistic €15/pass it is **~26 visits**, i.e. near-weekly flying.

The rest of the gap would have to come from:

- **Subscriptions.** Count only what you'd genuinely buy. NordVPN (~€100/yr) plus one more
  regularly-used sub gets you meaningfully toward it.
- **Insurance upgrades.** Worth maybe €50–100/yr unless you take 30+ day trips or ski.

**Ultra wins if:** you fly 8–10+ times a year through lounge-equipped airports, *and* you'd genuinely
pay for at least two of the subscriptions, *and* you travel outside EU roaming often enough for the
eSIM to matter.

**Ultra loses if:** your travel is a handful of trips a year and the driver is insurance.

---

## 9. Verdict

**Do not buy Ultra for the insurance.** That is the one justification the numbers clearly reject.
The medical cover you are paying €540/year for is the same €10M cover Premium provides for €120/year,
and a superior standalone policy costs €210. Buying Ultra for insurance means paying roughly
**4x the market rate for a product that is, on the specifics, weaker than a dedicated policy.**

And do not let cashback influence the decision at all — at €20–80/year on your spend, and identical
on Metal, it is noise.

### Recommended path

**Start with Metal (€155/yr paid annually).** It gives you the full €10M travel medical cover, €5,000
cancellation, spouse/partner inclusion, and the exact same cashback as Ultra — for about a third of
the price.

**Add a standalone annual travel policy (IATI or similar, ~€210/yr) only if** you take long trips,
travel to the US/Canada/Japan, or want a real claims process behind you. Total ~€365/yr, still under
Ultra, with better insurance.

**Upgrade to Ultra later, annually (€540), only if** you look back after 6–12 months on Metal and
find you're flying enough that lounge access alone would have paid for it. That is a question your
actual boarding passes answer better than any estimate — and Revolut lets you switch up at any time,
so there is no cost to waiting.

The one scenario where Ultra is straightforwardly right: you're a frequent flyer who already pays for
Priority Pass (~€430) and a couple of these subscriptions. Then Ultra absorbs those costs and the
insurance rides along free. **But that case is made by the lounges, not by the insurance — and from
what you've described, the lounges are the "nice to have," not the driver.**

---

## Caveats on these figures

Revolut's own domains were unreachable from the research environment, so all pricing and coverage
figures come from search results and third-party Spanish sources (Aug 2026). Revolut changed Ultra
pricing recently and Metal's price is reported inconsistently. **Confirm current prices and the full
policy documents in the app before committing**, particularly:

- Exact Metal price — sources report €13.99 and €15.99/mo, and €135 and €155/yr
- The in-app discounted lounge pass price for the airports you actually fly through (Revolut does not
  publish this; it varies by lounge, and lounge benefits have been trimmed before)
- Which partner subscriptions are actually live in Spain
- Travel insurance activation rules and pre-existing condition exclusions
- Whether the annual €540 Ultra rate is still offered
